# From Screen Time to Story Time

> *Entertainment content in today's world is based on addiction.*

We've all seen the infamous "iPad Kids." While there are plenty of benefits to teaching your child how to use modern technology — and a little time on the iPad can be a soothing, stimulating experience — it can become very easy (and tempting) to rely on ultra-captivating cheap content when it provides parents with much-needed quiet time.

After all, how many of us are chronically online, scrolling reels, watching YouTube videos? We're not immune to it either.

---

## The Algorithm Problem

Here's the bottom line: entertainment content in today's world is built on addiction. With modern technology, the recipe is already solved — shovel as much content as possible toward the user and reward the algorithm for finding that next video that will guarantee more scrolling.

It's no wonder that traditional entertainment like physical books is going by the wayside. Specially-crafted content is curated specifically for you with the sole purpose of keeping you entertained for as long as possible. A children's book can't compete with that — at least, not on the algorithm's terms.

**So we asked a different question.**

---

## A Different Kind of Engagement

Is there any way to use modern technology to provide a captivating experience that feels a little less like being force-fed by an algorithm?

What we found is that when you empower the audience to take a more substantive role in their entertainment, it becomes far more valuable. Everyone feels a sense of ownership and pays closer attention when they are the star of their own story.

> *When a child sees their own name, their own face, and their own world inside a children's book — that story isn't just entertainment. It's theirs.*

We didn't need a large-scale study or focus group to discover this. When my Dad read children's stories to his three kids before bed, he would replace characters' names with our own. He would act out what he was reading by changing his voice to match the tone of the story. Those weren't just bedtime stories — they were *our* stories.

**What if we could bring that kind of experience to everyone?**

---
